About

A crop insurer that misses a damaged field fails a farmer; a diagnostic tool that misses an infected cell fails a patient. In both cases the costly error is the one nobody flags. My work builds cascaded classifiers that hold a recall floor on the critical class: a cheap first model handles the easy cases, and anything uncertain goes to a stronger model or an expert.

The floor holds on data like the calibration set. It carries no guarantee once field conditions change, and that is the open problem I want to work on.

When a prediction system is calibrated on one distribution, what can still be guaranteed about its miss rate on another?

I trained first in pure mathematics, then in mathematics, economics and statistics. I am applying to PhD programs for 2027.

Read moreShow less

The tools I want to build on are selective prediction, cascaded classifiers and chance-constrained learning. More broadly, I am interested in models that can abstain, defer or adapt under uncertainty, and in how these mechanisms interact with the real decision systems they feed.

The same gap appears in education. In Benin's baccalaureate, a single zero in mathematics can eliminate a candidate whatever the overall result, so a missed weak student costs far more than a false alarm.

I am looking for PhD training in applied mathematics, machine learning or economics, to work on the theory of decision-aware learning under asymmetric risk.

Research

CascadeCropNet: crop damage assessment with a recall floor under peer review

With G. K. Gounou and S. K. Zimé · African School of Economics · since Oct 2023

A two-stage cascade for picture-based crop insurance. A light model screens smartphone images of maize fields; images it cannot clear go to a stronger model, with thresholds calibrated so that damaged fields are rarely missed.

23,804 field images, Kenya damaged-class recall about 0.97 on held-out data 13.2% fewer expert calls

Calibration and test splits are random, not spatial: the recall floor is validated empirically and has no guarantee outside the calibration distribution.

Read moreShow less
  • Data. The Eyes on the Ground dataset: 23,804 georeferenced smartphone images from smallholder maize farms in Kenya.
  • Design. Two stages, a Sentinel and an Expert. The light Sentinel sees every image; images it cannot clear with confidence go to the stronger Expert model. The Sentinel is trained under a hard recall constraint (damaged-class recall at least 0.95), enforced at checkpoint selection by threshold calibration. This is a heuristic, which raises the question of whether a chance-constrained formulation could give stronger guarantees.
  • Results. Damaged-class recall of about 0.97 on held-out data, with 13.2% fewer images sent to the Expert model. Under field and sensor corruption the Expert's metrics stay stable, while a flat single-stage baseline degrades by 17 to 35%.
  • Not shown yet. A spatial split, where whole areas are held out, would test the floor under the kind of shift an insurer meets in the field. That question is where my review below starts.

Submitted April 2026. Supervised by Dr Songbian Zimé. arXiv:2607.25468 · SSRN

Recall guarantees under distribution shift in progress

Literature review, Oct 2026

A review that treats the recall floor as a constraint in probability and maps what existing results can and cannot guarantee for cascaded classifiers. The hardest gap it identifies is shift with little information about the target distribution.

Read moreShow less

The review reads the recall floor as a constraint that must hold with high probability, then sorts the open questions into gaps. Two of them:

  • G2, composite cascade risk. How the errors of the stages combine. It does not arise in CascadeCropNet, because the expert stage there never rejects a case.
  • G3, shift with little target information. The hardest gap, and the one I want to work on during a PhD.

MalariaCascade preprint

With Michel Emel (AIMS Cameroon) · 2026

The same cascade design for malaria cell images (NIH dataset, 27,558 cells): about 96% recall on clean images, while a single network falls to 32.8% under heavy sensor noise. arXiv:2609.33005

Read moreShow less

A second application, to test whether the CascadeCropNet design carries over from field photos to medical images.

The data is the public NIH malaria dataset of thin blood smear cell images, half parasitized and half uninfected. Under severe sensor noise the cascade loses 2.4 points of parasitized-cell recall, while a single network falls to 0.33. A journal manuscript is in preparation.

Maize yields and climate

With Dr Jean-Claude Ahouangbenon · since Jul 2026

Forecasting maize yields from a commune-level panel for Benin, Burkina Faso and Togo (155 communes, 1984 to 2023). Rainfall-only models did not beat simple baselines; vegetation data comes next.

Read moreShow less

The panel holds about 217,000 records and is combined with satellite rainfall estimates (CHIRPS). Models are benchmarked against climatological baselines with rolling-origin cross-validation. So far, naive climatological forecasts remain hard to beat in these low-data smallholder settings. The project is led by Dr Ahouangbenon.

WIIRU Learn

Supervised by Dr Yaé Ulrich Gaba · working paper

Adaptive revision for technical-track baccalaureate students in Benin, using Bayesian Knowledge Tracing to reduce the risk of an eliminating zero in mathematics. Not deployed.

Read moreShow less

Part of the Technipreneur Programme. Written for Terminale students on the technical and industrial tracks (F, E, EA, BTP, IMI, OG, DPB and others), where zero scores in mathematics are most frequent.

Bayesian and Deep Knowledge Tracing estimate, skill by skill, what a student has mastered, updated after every answer. A retrieval-augmented generation pipeline produces practice questions grounded in the national curriculum. Revision is framed as a constrained sequential decision problem: keep the risk of an eliminating zero low. Status: working paper, not deployed.
