About

A crop insurer that misses a damaged field fails a farmer; a diagnostic tool that misses an infected cell fails a patient. In both cases the costly error is the one nobody flags. My work builds cascaded classifiers that hold a recall floor on the critical class: a cheap first model handles the easy cases, and anything uncertain goes to a stronger model or an expert.

The floor holds on data like the calibration set. It carries no guarantee once field conditions change, and that is the open problem I want to work on.

When a prediction system is calibrated on one distribution, what can still be guaranteed about its miss rate on another?

I trained first in pure mathematics, then in mathematics, economics and statistics. I am applying to PhD programs for 2027.

Research

CascadeCropNet: crop damage assessment with a recall floor under review

With G. K. Gounou and S. K. Zimé · African School of Economics · since Oct 2023

A two-stage cascade for picture-based crop insurance. A light model screens smartphone images of maize fields; images it cannot clear go to a stronger model, with thresholds calibrated so that damaged fields are rarely missed.

23,804 field images, Kenya damaged-class recall about 0.97 on held-out data 13.2% fewer expert calls

Calibration and test splits are random, not spatial: the recall floor is validated empirically and has no guarantee outside the calibration distribution.

Recall guarantees under distribution shift in progress

Literature review, Oct 2026

A review that treats the recall floor as a constraint in probability and maps what existing results can and cannot guarantee for cascaded classifiers. The hardest gap it identifies is shift with little information about the target distribution.

MalariaCascade preprint

With Michel Emel · 2026

The same cascade design for malaria cell images (NIH dataset, 27,558 cells): about 96% recall on clean images, while a single network falls to 32.8% under heavy sensor noise. arXiv:2609.33005

Maize yields and climate

With Dr Jean-Claude Ahouangbenon · since Jul 2026

Forecasting maize yields from a commune-level panel for Benin, Burkina Faso and Togo (155 communes, 1984 to 2023). Rainfall-only models did not beat simple baselines; vegetation data comes next.

Aguda digital history platform
